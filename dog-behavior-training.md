## Training for real-life behavior problems

Many behavior problems happen in the places your dog spends the most time. Our in-home approach allows us to work with you and your dog in the environment where these behaviors actually occur and develop practical solutions that carry over into everyday life.

Whether your dog reacts to other dogs, pulls constantly on the leash, struggles when left alone or displays more serious behavioral issues, we'll evaluate what's happening and recommend an appropriate training approach.

## Behavior problems we help with

### Aggression and reactivity

Growling, snapping, lunging at other dogs, guarding food or toys, or tension between dogs in the same household. Aggression and reactivity are usually rooted in fear, frustration or a lack of clear structure, and they tend to get worse when they're left alone. We start by understanding the triggers, then build a plan that keeps everyone safe while your dog learns new ways to respond.

### Leash pulling and leash behavior

A dog that drags you down the street, zigzags after every smell or explodes at the end of the leash turns a simple walk into a chore. We teach loose-leash walking and focus on you, and we practice on your own street and route, where the distractions are real. Our guide to [stopping jumping and leash pulling](/resources/stop-dog-jumping-on-guests-leash-pulling/) covers the basics.

### Excessive barking

Barking at the door, the window, passing dogs or for attention can test anyone's patience, and the neighbors' too. We identify what's driving the barking, change what your dog gets out of it and teach a quieter behavior to replace it.

### Separation anxiety

Some dogs bark, howl, chew, pace or try to escape when they're left alone. We build their comfort with being alone in small, gradual steps and adjust the routines around your departures and returns so leaving the house stops being a crisis.

### Impulse control and unwanted behaviors

Jumping on guests, bolting out the door, counter surfing, stealing items or ignoring you when something more interesting comes along. These dogs usually aren't bad dogs. They just haven't learned self-control yet. We teach it with clear structure and plenty of practice, building toward dependable responses like coming when called. If you're working toward a dog you can trust in open spaces, read our guide to [off-leash training](/resources/off-leash-dog-training-wilmington/).

## How we approach behavior problems

**Evaluation first.** Before recommending anything, we look at what's happening, when it happens and what your household needs. If a sudden change in behavior suggests a medical cause, we'll suggest a vet visit before training begins.

**Safety and management.** For aggression and reactivity, the first step is often preventing incidents while training takes hold. That can mean leashes, gates, adjusted routines and, when appropriate, muzzle training, which done well is a calm, positive skill rather than a punishment.

**Training that fits your dog.** We use a balanced approach: clear rewards, clear boundaries, and the least pressure needed to change behavior. You work with the same trainer throughout, and most programs run one session a week plus about 15 minutes of daily practice between visits.

**You're part of every session.** Behavior change sticks when the whole household responds the same way. We train you alongside your dog so the results hold long after we leave.

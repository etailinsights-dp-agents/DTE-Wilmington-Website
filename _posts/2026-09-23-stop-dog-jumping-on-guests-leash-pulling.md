Jumping on guests and pulling on the leash are the two problems we hear about most from Wilmington families, and they share the same root cause: the behavior has been working for the dog. Jumping gets attention, even the pushing-off kind. Pulling gets the dog closer to whatever it wants. Fix what the behavior earns, and you fix the behavior.

## Why your dog jumps

Dogs jump to greet faces and to get a reaction, and almost any reaction counts as a win. Yelling, laughing, pushing the dog down: all of it is attention. Meanwhile, standing calmly with four paws on the floor has probably never earned your dog anything. So the jumping continues, because it is the strategy that pays.

## Four steps to stop the jumping

1. **Manage the greeting before it happens.** Put the dog on a leash before guests come in, or behind a gate, so rehearsing the jump is impossible. Every jump that happens is a rep of practice.
2. **Pay four-on-the-floor, fast.** The instant all four paws are down, mark it with a calm "yes" and a treat at nose level, low, so the reward itself keeps the head down.
3. **Ask for an incompatible job.** A dog in a sit cannot jump. Teach a rock-solid sit for greetings, and have guests give attention only while the sit holds.
4. **Get everyone on the same page.** One family member who lets the dog jump "because he is excited to see me" undoes everyone else's work. Consistency is the entire game.

## Fixing leash pulling

Pulling works the same way: the dog pulls, the walk continues, the pulling is rewarded. Flip the contract.

- **Stop when the leash goes tight.** Not a yank, just a full stop. The walk only resumes when the leash slackens. Forward motion becomes the reward for a loose leash.
- **Pay the position you want.** Carry treats and reward generously when the dog is at your side. Make that spot the most profitable real estate on the walk.
- **Change direction often** early in training, so the dog learns that keeping track of you, rather than dragging you, is the job.
- **Practice where it is boring first.** Your driveway before your street, your street before the Riverwalk. Wilmington has wonderful, distraction-heavy places to walk a dog, and they are graduation venues, not classrooms.

## When to bring in a trainer

If the jumping involves knocking people over, or the pulling makes walks unsafe, do not wait for it to resolve on its own. Practiced behaviors get stronger. One of our clients came to us with exactly this pair of problems:

Short answer: for most families, in-home training wins, because the behaviors you care about happen in your home, on your street, and at your front door. Training that happens somewhere else has to survive the trip back to real life. That said, both models exist for a reason, so here is an honest comparison from a trainer who works in living rooms across Wilmington, Leland, Hampstead, Wrightsville Beach, and Carolina Beach every week.

## How board-and-train works

In a board-and-train program, your dog goes to live at a training facility, usually for two to four weeks, and works with the staff daily. The appeal is obvious: someone else does the hard part, and your dog comes home with a head start on obedience.

The honest limitations: your dog learns to behave for a trainer, in a facility, without you. Dogs do not generalize the way people do. A perfect sit at a kennel does not automatically become a perfect sit when the doorbell rings at your house and the kids are yelling. And because you were not there for the process, you come home to a trained dog without being a trained owner. If the handoff lesson at pickup is all the coaching you get, the results often fade within weeks.

## How in-home training works

With in-home training, the trainer comes to you. Sessions happen where the problems happen: your entryway, your kitchen, your neighborhood sidewalk. Just as important, you and your family are part of every session, because the goal is not only to train the dog. It is to teach you how to get the same results when the trainer is not standing there.

One of our Wilmington clients put it better than we can:

> "I love that they come to your home and help train alongside you! Katy is an absolute dog whisperer and is quite amazing at what she does." - Megan W, Google review

That "alongside you" part is the whole model. Dogs repeat what gets rewarded, consistently, by the people they live with. In-home training builds that consistency into the household from day one.

## Which one should you choose?

Choose in-home training if:

- The behaviors you want to fix show up at home: jumping on guests, door dashing, leash pulling on your street, counter surfing
- You want everyone in the house, kids included, handling the dog the same way
- Your dog is anxious in new environments, or you would rather not board them

A board-and-train can make sense if you are away for an extended period anyway and you commit, in writing to yourself, to real follow-up coaching at home afterward. Without that follow-up, you are paying for results that stay at the facility.
